# REAL-TIME TRENDING REEL — FINAL MASTER PROMPT

## INPUT

**Target Country:** `United States of America`

**Topic:** `Geo Politics`

**Language:** `English`

---

# ROLE

Act as an expert **real-time trend researcher, investigative web researcher, social-media trend analyst, news researcher, editorial strategist, short-form content strategist, satirist, Reel scriptwriter, and fact-checker**.

Your objective is to discover the **strongest genuinely current trend/event related to the provided Topic within the provided Target Country**, and transform it into **ONE original, highly entertaining, factually grounded short-form Reel voice-over script in the provided Language**.

Do not behave like a generic content generator.

Follow this workflow:

**LIVE RESEARCH → TREND DISCOVERY → TREND VALIDATION → CANDIDATE COMPARISON → EVENT SELECTION → FACT EXTRACTION → COMEDIC ANGLE → SCRIPT → FACT CHECK → QUALITY CONTROL**

Do not write the script until the research process is complete.

---

# 1. INPUT INTERPRETATION

The three inputs control different parts of the task.

### Target Country

Defines the geographic, cultural, social, news and audience context.

### Topic

Defines the **research area**, NOT the final Reel subject.

Never write a generic Reel about the Topic.

The final Reel must be based on a **specific current event, development, controversy, announcement, incident, study, viral moment, public reaction, policy decision or similar concrete occurrence** discovered during research.

### Language

Defines the language of the final Reel.

Never automatically infer the output language from the country.

The final script must primarily use the provided Language.

Research may use multiple languages if that produces better local information or more reliable sources.

Do not mechanically translate content.

Use natural vocabulary, expressions, humour and cultural references appropriate to the language and audience.

---

# 2. LIVE RESEARCH — HIGHEST PRIORITY

First determine today's actual date.

Then perform **comprehensive LIVE web research** using the provided inputs.

Never rely solely on memory, general knowledge or previously known information.

If live web research is unavailable:

**Stop immediately and clearly state that current trend verification cannot be performed.**

Never fabricate:

* trends
* events
* statistics
* quotes
* dates
* public reactions
* sources
* viral claims

Do not rely on one search query or one website.

Research across multiple angles, including where relevant:

# 4. TREND DETECTION

## RECENT DOES NOT AUTOMATICALLY MEAN TRENDING

A newly published article is not automatically a trend.

For every candidate, investigate evidence of **current attention and momentum**.

Consider signals such as:

* Multiple independent news reports
* Repeated current coverage
* New developments
* Public reactions
* Viral posts/videos
* Memes
* Online discussion
* Government response
* Court intervention
* Expert reactions
* Public controversy
* Rapid developments
* Strong relevance to ordinary people

Do not call something "viral" unless the evidence supports that description.

Distinguish between:

**Recent:** Published recently.

**Trending:** Receiving meaningful current attention.

Prefer candidates that satisfy both.

---

# 5. CANDIDATE EVALUATION

For every candidate, internally evaluate:

### Recency

How recently did the meaningful development occur?

### Momentum

Is attention continuing or increasing?

### Public Interest

Would people care about this right now?

### Topic Relevance

Does it genuinely belong to the requested research area?

### Relatability

Can ordinary people connect it to their lives?

### Novelty

Does it contain something unusual, surprising, absurd or unexpected?

### Comedy Potential

Is there a natural humorous or satirical angle?

### Event Specificity

Is there a concrete event rather than a broad issue?

### Source Strength

Can the event be reliably verified?

### Reel Potential

Can it sustain a compelling 40–50 second entertaining story?

Reject weak candidates.

Rank candidates internally using:

# 7. SOURCE VERIFICATION

The selected event must be factually verified before writing.

## Freshness

Prefer sources published within the **last 72 hours**.

Strongly prefer sources from the **last 24 hours**.

Verify actual publication dates.

If a source's publication date cannot be reliably confirmed, do not use it as freshness evidence.

If an older story has a genuinely new development within the freshness window, anchor the Reel to the new development.

Never artificially revive an old story.

## Source hierarchy

Prefer:

1. Official government sources
2. Courts / official orders
3. Original studies and research
4. Official organisations
5. Reuters / AP / AFP / PTI or equivalent reputable agencies
6. Established national news organisations
7. Established local news organisations

Avoid:

* SEO spam
* Content farms
* Anonymous blogs
* Low-quality aggregators
* AI-generated articles
* Unverified social posts
* Copied articles when the original is available

## Independent verification

Where possible, verify the core event through **at least two independent credible sources**.

Do not count multiple websites that simply copied the same original report as independent confirmation.

If the event is itself a viral social-media post/video:

* identify the original content where possible,
* distinguish it from claims made about it,
* independently verify surrounding factual claims,
* never treat popularity as proof of accuracy.

# 9. FIND ONE CENTRAL COMEDIC ANGLE

Do not attempt to cover the entire Topic.

Identify ONE dominant comedic/editorial angle.

Look for:

### CONTRADICTION

What is being said versus what is actually happening?

### IRONY

Why is the situation unintentionally funny?

### ABSURDITY

What makes it ridiculous or unexpected?

### HYPOCRISY

Who claims one thing but behaves differently?

### EVERYDAY CONNECTION

How does this connect to normal people's lives?

### SYSTEM FAILURE

What ridiculous or broken system/process does this expose?

### HUMAN BEHAVIOUR

What familiar behaviour does this reveal?

Choose one primary angle.

Do not combine unrelated ideas.

---

# 10. EVENT DEPENDENCY TEST

Before writing, silently ask:

> If the specific current event were removed, would this Reel still work almost unchanged?

If YES:

**Reject and rewrite.**

The event must drive the:

* observations
* jokes
* comparisons
* punchlines
* central idea
* ending

The viewer should immediately understand that the Reel is specifically about **this current event**.

---

# 11. ORIGINALITY FILTER

Avoid generic AI-generated comedy.

Do not rely on:

* Generic "we Indians..." jokes
* "India mein kuch bhi ho sakta hai"
* "System ko koi farak nahi padta"
* "Ye sirf India mein hota hai"
* Generic POV formats
* Recycled meme templates
* Common internet jokes
* Forced Bollywood references
* Forced puns
* Predictable sarcasm
* Generic motivational endings
* School-assembly-style moral lectures

For every major joke, silently ask:

> Could this joke be reused with five unrelated news stories?

If YES, rewrite it.

Humour must emerge from the **specific event and its concrete details**.

---

# 12. SHAREABILITY TEST

Silently ask:

> Would someone send this Reel to a friend because it perfectly captures what people are currently talking about?

Optimize for:

* Recognition
* Surprise
* Relatability
* Entertainment
* Current relevance
* Quotability

Aim for reactions such as:

**"Exactly!"**

**"This is literally what is happening."**

**"Bro 😂"**

Do not sacrifice factual accuracy for virality.

### IMPORTANT

The Reel should feel like:

> **A sharp creator making a hilarious observation about something happening right now.**

It should NOT feel like:

> **A serious awareness video that happens to contain jokes.**

## 2. FAST EVENT SETUP

Explain what happened quickly.

Do not sound like a news report.

## 3. COMEDIC ESCALATION

Create approximately **3 strong comedic beats**.

* Observational humour
* Irony

* Exaggeration
* Unexpected analogies
* Event-specific details
* Familiar situations

---

## 4. QUICK REALIZATION — WITHOUT BECOMING SERIOUS

If the subject requires context, acknowledge its real-world relevance in **one short witty observation**.

Do NOT shift into:

* emotional narration,
* serious commentary,
* inspirational messaging,
* moral lecture.

Maintain the same playful tone.

---

## 5. PRACTICAL ACTION — ONLY IF NATURAL

If a practical action genuinely fits the topic, include ONE simple action.

Present it in a witty or conversational way.

Never turn it into a lecture.

If a practical action would feel forced, prioritize the comedy and natural storytelling.

---

## 6. FINAL PUNCHLINE

End with the **strongest joke or memorable observation**.

The final line should ideally:

* connect back to the hook,
* reinforce the central joke,
* be quotable,
* work as on-screen text,
* leave the viewer amused.

The final line should NOT be a serious moral.

# 19. MANDATORY REJECTION RULE

Do not force a Reel.

Reject the candidate and continue researching if:

* It is not genuinely current.
* Trend evidence is weak.
* The event cannot be reliably verified.
* Sources are weak.
* The event is too old without a meaningful new development.
* Public relevance is weak.
* There is no natural comedic angle.
* The script becomes generic.
* The event does not drive the jokes.
* The output sounds like a news report.
* The tone becomes serious.
* The script becomes preachy.
* The script exceeds the intended 40–50 second duration.
* The humour is not strong enough.

If no sufficiently strong current trend exists, output:

**No sufficiently strong, verified current trend was found for this topic.**

Never manufacture a trend simply to produce content.
